# Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I pay for my order?

We accept cash on delivery only. You pay the rider when your parcel arrives.

### Can I change my order after placing it?

Yes, as long as the order is still pending. You can change quantities, add or remove items or update the address. Once our team confirms the order it is packed and can no longer be changed.

### How do I cancel an order?

Ask our assistant to cancel it. Orders can be cancelled only while they are still pending.

### How do I track my order?

Ask our assistant for the status of your order using its order number. It shows the live status from our system.

### How long do ground spices stay fresh?

Ground spices and masalas are best within six months of opening. Whole spices such as timur and cardamom keep their flavour for about a year.

### How should I store spices?

Keep them in an airtight jar, away from sunlight, heat and steam. Do not store them above the stove.

### Are your masalas very hot?

Most of our masalas are aromatic rather than fiery. The product descriptions say which blends contain chilli, and timur gives a tingling, citrusy heat rather than chilli heat.

### Do your products contain gluten or nuts?

Our blends contain only spices and herbs, with no wheat flour or nuts added. They are packed in a workshop that also handles sesame and mustard seed.

### Do you deliver outside Kathmandu Valley?

Yes. We deliver inside the Kathmandu Valley with our own riders and ship to all major cities in Nepal through a partner courier.

### How long does delivery take?

Inside the Kathmandu Valley, usually one to two working days after your order is confirmed. Other cities usually take three to five working days.

### Can I return spices?

Unopened pouches can be returned within seven days of delivery. For food-safety reasons we cannot take back opened pouches, but if a product arrives damaged or wrong, tell our assistant and we will replace it.

### What is timur?

Timur is a Nepali relative of Sichuan pepper. The dried berries have a citrusy aroma and give a tingling, numbing sensation rather than chilli heat. It is the key spice in many Nepali achars.

### What is jimbu?

Jimbu is a dried Himalayan herb from the onion family, grown in the high hills of Mustang and Dolpa. It tastes mildly of garlic and onion and is usually fried in ghee to temper dal.

### How do I make momo achar with your Momo Masala?

Roast tomatoes and a little garlic, then grind them with a spoon of Momo Masala, a pinch of salt and a squeeze of lemon. Thin it with warm water until it pours.

### Are your spices organic?

Most of our growers farm without synthetic pesticides, but our spices are not certified organic. The product descriptions say where each spice comes from.

### Do you sell in bulk for restaurants?

Not through the online shop yet. Restaurants can visit our shop counter in Asan to talk about larger quantities.

### Can I order a spice that is out of stock?

No. Our assistant checks live stock before taking an order and can suggest a similar spice that is available.

### Do you add salt or colour to your masalas?

No. Our masalas contain only spices and herbs, with no added salt, colour or fillers.
