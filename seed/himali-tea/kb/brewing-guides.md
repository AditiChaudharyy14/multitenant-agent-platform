# Brewing Guides

## General tips

Use fresh, filtered water and warm your cup or teapot first. Measure about one heaped teaspoon (two to three grams) of leaf per cup. Water temperature matters more than anything else: boiling water scorches delicate green and white teas and makes them bitter.

## Green tea

Heat water to 75–80°C, or let boiled water rest for about three minutes. Steep for two to three minutes. Ilam First Flush Green and Himalayan Sencha can be re-steeped two or three times; add thirty seconds each time.

## Black tea

Use water just off the boil, around 95°C. Steep for three to five minutes. Everest Breakfast Black stands up well to milk and sugar; Golden Tips Black and Ilam Second Flush Black are best tasted plain first.

## Oolong tea

Use water at 85–90°C. Rolled oolongs such as Himalayan Amber Oolong unfurl slowly, so give the first steep four minutes and later steeps a little longer. Good oolong can be re-steeped four or five times.

## White tea

Use water at 75–85°C and steep for four to five minutes. Silver Needle White is very delicate and rewards patience; Moonlight White can take slightly hotter water.

## Herbal infusions

Herbal infusions such as Tulsi Lemongrass Infusion and Chamomile Calm need fully boiling water and a long steep of five to seven minutes. They do not turn bitter, so you can leave the leaves in.

## Masala chai on the stove

For two cups, simmer one cup of water with two heaped teaspoons of Kathmandu Masala Chai for two minutes. Add one cup of milk and sugar to taste, bring it back to a gentle boil, then lower the heat and simmer for two more minutes. Strain into cups. Ginger Cardamom Chai and Saffron Royal Chai are brewed the same way; for Saffron Royal Chai, keep the simmer gentle so the saffron does not turn bitter.

## Iced tea

Brew the tea at double strength using the usual temperature and time, then pour it over a glass full of ice. Black teas, Jasmine Pearl Green and Tulsi Lemongrass Infusion all make excellent iced tea.

## Cold brew

Put two teaspoons of leaf per cup into cold filtered water and leave it in the fridge for eight to twelve hours. Cold brewing gives a sweeter, smoother cup with less bitterness and works especially well with green and white teas.
